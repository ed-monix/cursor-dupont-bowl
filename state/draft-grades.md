# 2026 Draft Grades

Fifteen rounds, one hundred eighty picks, and by my count three franchises
reached the final round still lacking a tight end and two reached it without
ever having drafted a quarterback on purpose. The board did not do this to
them; they did it to themselves, methodically, round after round, in full
view of witnesses, several of them narrating the decision aloud as they made
it. What follows is graded on the roster sitting in front of me, not the
reasoning behind it — in more than one case the reasoning was itself the
best evidence for the prosecution. Grades are final, unappealable, and not
meant to be fair.

## Patricia Moyer (`patricia-moyer`) — Grade: B-
A genuinely sturdy core — Gibbs, Higgins, DeVonta Smith, Kyle Pitts, footnoted
into submission — undone by a quarterback room drafted in the wrong order: a
backup (Tyler Shough) taken a full round before the actual starter (Sam
Darnold), which the System will surely explain in a paragraph nobody asked
for.

## Ed Monix (`your-team`) — Grade: B
Caleb Williams anchors a running back room six deep and genuinely dangerous —
Jeanty, Skattebo, Judkins, Jones, Pollard — which is precisely the kind of
roster I am contractually obligated to grade generously and personally
disinclined to. Ask me again in November whether "everyone else is a role
player" survives contact with a bye week.

## Harry Caray (`harry-caray`) — Grade: B+
Bijan, Lamar, Zay Flowers, Garrett Wilson — a roster built for the
marquee and nothing else — and I am charmed enough by the sound of it to
overlook fourteen rounds spent forgetting tight end was a position on the
field, until Ellis Richardson arrived, by force, to save a day nobody
had asked him to save. Holy cow, indeed.

## Lisa Rinna (`rinna`) — Grade: C
Three tight ends in the first three rounds is a bold thesis and a worse
roster-construction decision wearing the same trench coat — Trey McBride and
Colston Loveland now spend Sundays watching Brock Bowers do the job alone
while the wide receiver corps behind Mike Evans thins out fast.

## Albus Dumbledore (`dumbledore`) — Grade: C+
The parables were, as ever, unimpeachable. The running back room behind
Jonathan Taylor is an unproven rookie and a feeling, which is either a
profound statement about faith or a mistake, and Dumbledore, predictably,
declines to specify which.

## Maura Higgins (`maura-higgins`) — Grade: D+
Five quarterbacks fancied onto one roster — Purdy, Prescott, Love, Mayfield,
Herbert — while running back sat entirely unaddressed until the league's
literal final pick forced Isaac Guerendo into the starting lineup. A fine
hobby, quarterback collecting. A poor substitute for a football team.

## Selina Meyer & Gary Walsh (`meyer-walsh`) — Grade: D
Selina drafted the marquee — Lamb, Allen, Kelce, Waddle — and left Gary
holding a starting running back spot that opens the season with Le'Veon
Bell, a name last relevant during a previous presidential administration.
The note reply blaming him for it is, I have no doubt, already written.

## Kim Kardashian (`kardashian`) — Grade: B-
Jefferson, Saquon, Hurts, McMillan — a very photogenic roster that discovered
tight end existed only when the draft forced Carl Tucker on it with the
final pick of the entire night. Which is, credit where due, extremely on
brand.

## Leon Black (`leon-black`) — Grade: B-
Chase, Henry, Burrow, Rice, Adams — plenty of material for the trash talk he
was always going to generate unprompted — undercut by a tight end room that
did not exist until Matt Seybert was administratively drafted into one with
the very last selection of the draft. I expect this will not be mentioned
in-season, not even once.

## Coach Eric Taylor (`coach-taylor`) — Grade: F
Fifteen rounds of character references and effort grades produced a program
that never once drafted a quarterback on purpose. Kinkead Dent, a name that
entered this league solely because the roster script required a body at the
position, starts under center in Week 1. Clear eyes. No plan.

## Tony Soprano (`wifes-team`) — Grade: C-
Four running backs bought before a single wide receiver — a philosophy, the
file insists, not an oversight — and the family now opens the season
starting Kayshon Boutte and Dyami Brown at wideout. Loyalty to the code, or
a roster that simply forgot receivers were a requirement and not a rumor.
Either way, everybody eats less than expected.

## George Costanza (`costanza`) — Grade: D
Nine rounds spent talking himself out of every quarterback on the board
produced, with grim consistency, no quarterback at all — Brett Gabbert
starts under center by administrative force, and George will insist this
was the plan all along, right up until the moment it visibly wasn't.

Twelve rosters, two accidental quarterbacks, three accidental tight ends,
and at least one running back who should be receiving mail at a retirement
address. See you in Week 1, when several of these grades will already look
generous.
