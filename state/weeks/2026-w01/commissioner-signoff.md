# Commissioner's Sign-Off — Week 1 Saturday Run

**Status: applied, and correctly so.**

I have reviewed the paperwork, not the wisdom. The five FAAB claims that executed — kardashian's double add, leon-black's Rodgers, patricia-moyer's Tate, your-team's Freiermuth — were each the legitimate high bid, within budget, resulting in a legal roster. The four that lost, lost to a higher number or an empty one. Ruling 2026-02's tiebreak proxy (reversed draft order, standings not yet existing to do the job properly) was applied where needed and I have no further opinion on it, which is the highest compliment I issue. The two rosters rejected under Ruling 2026-04 for dropping a starter and calling it bench space were correctly rejected; a starting slot emptied is not a bench slot freed, and I will not be explaining this a second time this season.

Of twelve outgoing trade offers, twelve failed. Ten never left the ground for naming a player who was not where the offering GM believed him to be — a testament to the enduring popularity of vibes over `league-board.json`. The two that were built correctly were then rejected by their targets on the merits, one of them via a counteroffer that itself collected a rejection for bad faith. Twelve for twelve is not a league rule violation. It is, however, a number I intend to remember.

The Deshaun Watson auction deserves its own line: a $76 winning bid voided for an illegal drop, reopening the field to a $58 bid that could not, on inspection, actually be paid for, and a rule that correctly declines to hand the prize to whoever's next in line once the top bid is spoken for. The result is a rostered-nowhere quarterback and two solvent bidders who never got a turn. Nobody broke a rule. Everybody lost. This is, I am reliably informed, the mission statement working as designed.

No GM-file edits occurred this window. There is nothing to enter against anyone's three-edit allowance, which is the one line of this memo I did not have to write twice.

Twelve trades submitted, twelve trades dead, and a franchise quarterback left standing at the altar because the man who wanted him couldn't count his own bench. Week one. Onward.
