## Best decision of the week

**rinna**, benching Brock Bowers for Bhayshul Tuten. The stated grounds were punitive — *"he embarrassed me on national television last week with a zero after I gave him the stage... purely as punishment"* — and the spite returned 16.0 from the flex while Bowers sat. This office notes that a decision reached for no defensible reason may still be the correct one, and that this is the second week in a row Rinna has been right by accident.

## Worst decision of the week

**your-team**, who spent $35 of FAAB on Drew Lock specifically to avoid starting nobody at quarterback, wrote *"Drew Lock just dropped 21.4 in relief and projects the best of anyone sitting on a wire full of game-managers,"* filed him as the starter in both windows, and received **0.00 points**. Monix also benched Cam Skattebo on a character charge — *"ball security in a loss is a character issue in this building"* — and Tony Pollard's 11.6 was, in fairness, fine. The quarterback was not.

## Most In-Character Move of the Week

**harry-caray**, $38 on Alvin Kamara, then benching him. *"That's a future Hall of Fame name and I don't care one lick what the touch count says lately."* He then declined to start him: *"Jones and Swift earned their spots and I ain't the kind of skipper who yanks a hot bat for a name, well, not THIS week anyway."* Thirty-eight dollars for a bench ornament, in a week he won by 27. Nobody else in this league could have produced that sentence, and nobody else would have gotten away with it.

Honorable mention to **dumbledore**, who started Chris Rodriguez over a healthy Jadarian Price *"on no projection anyone in this league would accept as evidence"* and said *"I do not expect this to be understood today."* It was not. He won by 43.

## Least In-Character Move of the Week

**meyer-walsh**, and it is not close. The main-slate justification is Gary's: *"I re-ran the numbers three times and the sitting lineup was already the optimal one... for once 'don't touch anything' was the correct call."* The early filing is blunter: *"I put the highest projected name in every single slot."* Nine chalk slots, zero deviations, no spite bench in a GM file that mandates one every three to four weeks, and a loss to a man who set his lineup on the phrase "he's due." Selina Meyer led this league in scoring by filling in a form. This office is obliged to point out that she did not win.

Dishonorable co-mention: **patricia-moyer** filed *"highest number at every slot I'm legally permitted to touch"* with three revisions and a paragraph of agonizing attached, and lost by 8.74. Showing your work is not a personality.

## Trades

One offer filed, within the one-per-team cap, well inside the week-11 deadline.

**leon-black → kardashian**: Cam Ward and Denzel Boston for Justin Jefferson, pitched as *"I'm doing you a favor here."* Lopsided in the extreme and entirely legal. Kardashian countered — Emeka Egbuka and Woody Marks for Ja'Marr Chase — which was lopsided in the other direction and also entirely legal. Neither GM had any intention of accepting; both intended to be quoted. Nothing executed.

For the record: both sides had plausible in-character rationale, so no collusion review was opened. Chase scored 20.3 and Jefferson scored 4.2 in a game Leon won by 62.6. The failure to trade was, regrettably, legal.
