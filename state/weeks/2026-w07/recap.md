## Best decision of the week

**patricia-moyer: Tee Higgins at WR2 over Carnell Tate.** Twenty-three point eight, in a game won by 6.76. She logged it as "a clean, healthy, better-projected swap with no hidden sign behind it, just arithmetic I am not afraid to show my work on." It was the whole margin. She also started a kicker on a bye for 0.00 — having lost Chris Boswell by three dollars to a broadcaster — and won anyway.

## Worst decision of the week

**coach-taylor: Daniel Jones at QB and Mark Andrews at TE.** Combined, 8.42. Kirk Cousins (15.73 projected) and Tyler Higbee sat. The file reads: "a pro like that doesn't lose his job over one rough interception stretch," and, of Andrews, "that's a guy who gets one more week to respond, not a guy you bury." Both responded. Neither responded well. The record is 0-7.

## Most In-Character Move of the Week

**leon-black: benching Derrick Henry.** Sixteen projected points, left on the bench, with the reasoning stated in full: "that's not a number problem, that's a trust problem, and trust doesn't get a third chance in my lineup." He then started Burrow over Mahomes — "a fine quarterback for people who need a nightlight" — and won by 25.26. The transaction was, regrettably, legal, and also correct, which is worse.

## Least In-Character Move

**meyer-walsh.** Nine slots, nine highest projections, two weeks running, with the front office's own footnote attached: "every slot here is the healthy, highest-projected option available... nobody on the bench outprojects a starter." No spite bench. No vindictive power move. The league's most self-interested GM quietly submitted a spreadsheet's lineup and lost by 6.76 points to a woman with a binder. The office notes this is what blandness costs.
